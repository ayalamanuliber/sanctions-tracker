# AI Vortex Legal AI Risk

## Endgame Product and Execution Master Plan

**Status:** Revised after strategic review; awaiting founder approval before implementation  
**Scope:** Website first, shared intelligence layer second, MCP and extension distribution surfaces next  
**Product thesis:** Public evidence is free. Users pay for convenience, continuity, private work, controlled branding, collaboration, automation, and institutional deployment.

---

## 1. Executive Product Decision

AI Vortex should become the source-linked intelligence and workflow layer for legal AI risk.

It is not three disconnected products. It is one evidence system delivered through three interfaces:

1. **Website:** find, understand, compare, organize, and share the public record.
2. **MCP:** reason with the record inside the user's preferred AI environment.
3. **Chrome extension:** retrieve and capture relevant evidence where legal work is already happening.

The website must be built first because it is the universal public surface, the SEO/AEO engine, the canonical record, the artifact renderer, and the trust layer used by the MCP and extension.

The initial business model should remain deliberately simple:

- **Free:** complete public intelligence and genuinely useful branded work products.
- **Professional:** approximately $20 initially, subject to testing, for clean exports, continuity, higher limits, private workflows, MCP/extension convenience, and early access.
- **Firm:** seats, shared workspaces, firm branding, governance, and support.
- **Services:** implementation, policy, training, workflow design, vendor review, and custom intelligence engagements.

The product must not force conversion by degrading free utility. It should become unavoidable by allowing high-quality AI Vortex records and artifacts to circulate throughout legal work.

## 2. Category Definition

### Product category

**Source-linked legal AI risk intelligence and review infrastructure.**

### Product promise

AI Vortex helps legal professionals find relevant public precedent, distinguish proven facts from allegations, compare reported conduct, and produce defensible research, review, policy, and training outputs.

### The three interface promises

- **Website:** Understand the record.
- **MCP:** Reason with the record.
- **Extension:** Use the record where the work happens.

### What AI Vortex is not

- Not a sanctions prediction engine.
- Not an AI-authorship detector.
- Not a substitute for Westlaw, Lexis, Bloomberg Law, PACER, or primary-source review.
- Not a generic legal chatbot.
- Not an accusation engine against counsel, litigants, vendors, or courts.
- Not a usage-adjusted product-safety ranking unless defensible denominator data later exists.
- Not a legal-news publication with an unfocused scope.

## 3. Non-Negotiable Product Principles

1. **Evidence before prose.** Every material conclusion must make its evidentiary basis inspectable.
2. **Public evidence stays useful for free.** Search, case records, sources, basic comparisons, and branded artifacts must solve real work.
3. **Charge for leverage, not access to public facts.** Monetize identity, continuity, volume, privacy, automation, collaboration, and implementation.
4. **Role-neutral workflow language.** Prefer review-ready, filing-ready, responsible reviewer, exception report, and verification record over hierarchy-specific language.
5. **No unsupported attribution.** A discrepancy is not proof of AI use, intent, fraud, or misconduct.
6. **Observed signals are not incidence rates.** Every chart and comparison must communicate denominator and coverage limitations.
7. **The primary source wins.** AI Vortex summaries accelerate review but never replace the underlying order, filing, opinion, or disciplinary record.
8. **Progressive disclosure.** Give the short answer first, then evidence, detail, exports, and advanced controls.
9. **One canonical record.** Website, MCP, extension, API, exports, alerts, and repositories must use the same stable object and IDs.
10. **Artifacts should elevate the user.** A free branded packet must still be credible enough to take into a meeting.
11. **Trust is a feature.** Methodology, correction history, provenance, uncertainty, and limitations are visible product surfaces.
12. **Do not build complexity before repeated demand.** Prove records, retrieval, comparison, artifacts, saving, and monitoring before enterprise sprawl.

## 4. Audience and Job Architecture

### 4.1 Litigators

Primary jobs:

- Find comparable sanctions and correction decisions.
- Review a filing for citation, quotation, and proposition issues.
- Understand aggravating and mitigating conduct.
- Prepare a neutral meet-and-confer or escalation record.
- Build a source-linked packet for a responsible reviewer.

Default product path:

`Search -> Case record -> Compare -> Filing review -> Review packet -> Save/follow`

### 4.2 Judges and clerks

Primary jobs:

- Locate comparable decisions and procedural responses.
- Distinguish allegation, admission, inference, and adjudicated finding.
- Compare corrective behavior and proportional responses.
- Preserve neutral language and primary-source support.

Default product path:

`Court/judge search -> Comparable records -> Neutral discrepancy matrix -> Source appendix`

Guardrail: never present advocacy-oriented suggested sanctions or infer AI use without evidence.

### 4.3 Law-firm risk, knowledge, ethics, and innovation teams

Primary jobs:

- Convert public incidents into concrete controls.
- Audit policies against known failure modes.
- Train lawyers using real source-linked examples.
- Track courts, judges, tools, and failure modes.
- Preserve an auditable review process.

Default product path:

`Dashboard -> Control maturity -> Policy gap -> Templates -> Alerts -> Shared workspace`

### 4.4 Associates and paralegals

Primary jobs:

- Verify citations, quotes, pincites, and proposition support.
- Record exceptions without producing a second wall of text.
- Escalate unresolved items efficiently.

Default product path:

`Filing gate -> Compact ledger -> Exception report -> Responsible review`

### 4.5 Researchers, academics, reporters, and students

Primary jobs:

- Search a reproducible corpus.
- Inspect taxonomy and source lineage.
- Export structured data and citations.
- Understand coverage gaps and correction history.

Default product path:

`Advanced search -> Dataset view -> Record history -> CSV/JSON -> Methodology citation`

### 4.6 Legal technology vendors

Primary jobs:

- Find public records where a product is explicitly named.
- Separate tool attribution from workflow failure.
- Understand buyer, court, and insurer concerns.
- Improve controls, documentation, and product design.

Default product path:

`Tool page -> Explicit mentions -> Failure dimensions -> Buyer questions -> Vendor assessment`

Guardrail: no comparative failure-rate claims without usage denominators.

### 4.7 General counsel and legal departments

Primary jobs:

- Set outside-counsel expectations.
- Review vendor and internal AI controls.
- Build escalation and incident-response procedures.
- Receive concise evidence-backed assurance artifacts.

### 4.8 Insurers and risk professionals

Primary jobs:

- Identify recurring control failures.
- Evaluate control maturity.
- Build underwriting or loss-control questions.
- Separate monetary sanctions, fees, damages, and unknown financial consequences.

### 4.9 Bar associations, law schools, and CLE providers

Primary jobs:

- Create real-case exercises and teaching materials.
- Generate instructor guides, hypotheticals, and assessment questions.
- Track evolving court expectations.

### 4.10 Consultants and multi-client providers

Primary jobs:

- Maintain separate client workspaces and brands.
- Reuse evidence and templates without mixing client material.
- Generate client-ready reports repeatedly.
- Transfer or share workspaces with clients.

This audience should eventually receive a dedicated multi-client plan rather than being forced into a conventional firm account.

### 4.11 First-session activation and onboarding

The product must define activation as a completed piece of useful legal work, not account creation or page consumption.

#### Activation outcomes by audience

- **Litigator:** finds one comparable matter, opens a primary source, and creates a comparison or review packet.
- **Judge/clerk:** finds an analogous decision, confirms its posture and source, and saves or exports a neutral comparison.
- **Risk/compliance professional:** selects a failure mode and generates a source-backed control checklist or policy-gap artifact.
- **Associate/paralegal:** starts a verification ledger and records at least one verified or escalated item.
- **Researcher:** creates a reproducible filtered dataset and exports a source list or structured result set.
- **Vendor:** finds explicit product mentions, inspects attribution evidence, and creates a defensible risk/control brief.
- **General counsel/insurer:** identifies a relevant control pattern and generates an assessment checklist.
- **Consultant:** creates a client repository and produces a branded or AI Vortex-branded deliverable.

#### Activation design rules

- Target first useful outcome in less than five minutes.
- Allow search, source inspection, comparison preview, and initial artifact generation without an account.
- Request account creation only when the user saves, follows, shares privately, or needs persistence.
- Show audience-relevant example searches before typing.
- Provide prebuilt repository and packet templates.
- Put “Use this case” actions on case pages: compare, add to packet, build checklist, save, or follow.
- Use sample packets to demonstrate the finished outcome before asking users to configure one.
- Ask no more than the minimum tailoring questions required to avoid a misleading result.
- A returning authenticated user lands on active work, recent repositories, alerts, and unfinished reviews rather than the generic public homepage.

#### Activation instrumentation

- Record time to first search, source open, comparison, artifact, save, and follow.
- Record which example, role path, case page, or external referral produced activation.
- Segment activation by audience, acquisition source, device, jurisdiction, and first entry-page family.
- Treat an account with no completed work product as unactivated.

## 5. The Canonical AI Vortex Record

Every interface must use a stable `AI Vortex Record` with versioned provenance.

- Stable record ID.
- Canonical slug.
- Canonical case or matter name.
- Alternative names.
- Citation.
- Docket number.
- Court.
- Judge or decision-maker.
- Jurisdiction and country.
- Decision and event dates.

### Procedural context

- Proceeding type.
- Procedural posture.
- Party/user type: lawyer, firm, pro se, expert, government, vendor, other.
- Practice area.
- Court level and system.

### AI attribution

- AI-use status: admitted, proven, inferred, alleged, disputed, unspecified, or unknown.
- Named tool.
- Attribution source.
- Attribution confidence.
- Exact supporting source location.

### Conduct and consequence

- Conduct description.
- Failure modes.
- Legal or ethical issues.
- Outcome.
- Sanction category.
- Monetary consequence type.
- Amount, currency, and amount status.
- Filing, case, professional, disciplinary, and referral consequences.

### Behavioral dimensions

- Knowledge or notice.
- Repetition.
- Correction behavior.
- Candor.
- Supervision.
- Reliance on paid/legal-specific tools.
- Harm to client, court, opponent, or process.
- Aggravating factors.
- Mitigating factors.

### Reasoning and controls

- Plain-English summary.
- Key judicial reasoning.
- Short compliant excerpts with pincites.
- Why the record matters.
- Policy gaps.
- Controls that could have prevented or reduced the problem.
- Comparable dimensions used for related-record retrieval.

### Provenance and review

- Primary sources.
- Secondary sources.
- Foundational database attribution.
- Field-level source references where available.
- Source hierarchy/tier.
- Import date.
- Last reviewed date.
- Reviewer status.
- Classification confidence.
- Allegation/adjudication boundary.
- Record version.
- Correction history.
- Related records.

### Required schema rules

- Financial amounts must distinguish sanctions, fees, costs, damages, settlement, request, fine, and other consequence.
- Unknown must not be encoded as zero.
- Alleged conduct must never be rendered as adjudicated fact.
- Tool-unspecified records must not be assigned to a vendor.
- Every generated artifact must include record IDs and retrieval date.
- Every API response must include schema version and source coverage.

### AEO answer blocks

Every entity page should include concise, source-linked answers to real questions, followed by the inspectable record.

### Technical requirements

- Canonical URLs.
- XML sitemap partitioning when scale requires it.
- Structured data matching visible content.
- Stable server-rendered answer content.
- Descriptive titles and metadata.
- Source and correction dates.
- Fast mobile rendering.
- No thin doorway pages.

### Programmatic publication and indexing policy

Entity pages are published for users before they are necessarily indexable for search engines.

#### Index eligibility

An entity page may be indexed only when it has:

- A canonical resolved entity and stable URL.
- Meaningful unique visible content.
- At least one verified primary or authoritative source.
- A direct answer block supported by that source.
- Accurate status, provenance, and last-reviewed information.
- Useful connected records, analysis, or navigation beyond a single repeated database row.
- No unresolved identity collision likely to mislead users.

#### Sparse-page handling

- Sparse judge, court, tool, topic, and jurisdiction pages remain accessible but `noindex` until they meet the threshold.
- Pages that never add unique value should redirect to the best parent entity or filtered record set.
- Entity aliases resolve to one canonical page.
- Misspellings and court abbreviations may be search aliases but do not receive separate indexable pages.
- Filter combinations are `noindex` by default unless promoted into a curated entity or question page.
- Sort, pagination, audience, display, and tracking parameters never create independent canonical pages.

#### Quality monitoring

- Maintain an index-eligibility score based on source, review, answer, uniqueness, and connected-record coverage.
- Automatically remove a page from index eligibility if its only source breaks or its identity becomes disputed.
- Audit orphan pages, duplicate titles, duplicate answer blocks, and crawlable parameter growth.
- Partition sitemaps by page family and include only canonical index-eligible URLs.

### Formal answer-block schema

Every major entity page should expose one reusable answer object:

- `direct_answer`
- `answer_scope`
- `ai_attribution_status`
- `outcome`
- `financial_consequence`
- `evidence_basis`
- `important_limitation`
- `last_reviewed`
- `source_ids`

Supported answer patterns should initially include:

- What happened in this matter?
- Was AI use proven, admitted, inferred, alleged, or unknown?
- What sanction or other consequence was imposed?
- What did this court or judge say?
- Which matters involve this failure mode?
- What controls could have prevented or reduced the problem?
- Which records are comparable, and on what dimensions?

The same answer object should power the page introduction, API, MCP readout, structured data, report introduction, and answer-engine responses without allowing those surfaces to invent different facts.

- Taxonomy definitions.
- Allegation-only treatment.
- Financial methodology.
- Correction process.
- Dataset limitations.
- Update cadence.

### Record-level trust surfaces

- Source coverage.
- Verification status.
- Last reviewed.
- Classification confidence.
- AI attribution status.
- Correction/version history.
- Report issue action.

### Editorial and data operations

The corpus is an operated product, not a static import.

#### Intake channels

- Scheduled synchronization from the foundational dataset.
- Official court, disciplinary, and regulatory sources.
- AI Vortex independent research.
- User-submitted cases, orders, rules, sources, and corrections.
- Monitored updates to existing records.

#### Processing pipeline

1. Ingest candidate with source snapshot and origin.
2. Resolve identity and deduplicate.
3. Extract factual fields with source locations.
4. Classify allegation/adjudication and AI-attribution status.
5. Classify conduct, outcome, financial category, and controls.
6. Run automated validation and contradiction checks.
7. Place risk-sensitive or low-confidence fields in human review.
8. Publish with visible review status.
9. Monitor sources and accept corrections.
10. Version every material change.

#### Review queues and priority

- Highest priority: new adjudicated matters, severe outcomes, broken primary sources, disputed identity, monetary changes, and public corrections.
- Medium priority: new secondary coverage, taxonomy enrichment, relationships, and historical additions.
- Lower priority: cosmetic metadata and low-impact enrichment.
- Allegation-only and product-attribution records require elevated review before prominent use.

#### Service levels to define before launch

- Target cadence for foundational-dataset synchronization.
- Target time from verified new matter to public record.
- Correction acknowledgment time.
- High-severity correction resolution target.
- Broken-primary-source repair target.
- Re-review cadence for prominent and heavily visited records.

#### Operational ownership

- Every pipeline stage must have an owner, queue, status, and audit event.
- Automated extraction never represents itself as human verification.
- Independent AI Vortex additions must be distinguishable from foundational imports.
- Changes from upstream must be diffed so AI Vortex enrichment is not silently overwritten.
- Editorial guidelines, taxonomy definitions, and exception decisions must be versioned.

### Security and privacy baseline

- Public workflows should not require confidential input.
- Private analysis requires explicit consent, retention disclosure, and deletion controls.
- Secrets and organization integrations remain server-side.
- Logs must avoid capturing document content unnecessarily.
- Workspace isolation and authorization require security tests before team launch.

## 19. Technical Product Architecture

### Core services

- Versioned record store.
- Source/provenance store.
- Search index.
- Relationship/related-record service.
- Artifact generator.
- Authentication and billing.
- Repository/workspace service.
- Watchlist/alert service.
- MCP server.
- Extension API.
- Analytics event pipeline.

### API requirements

- Stable IDs and schema versions.
- Consistent filters across website, MCP, extension, and exports.
- Exact-match and fallback metadata.
- Pagination and deterministic sorting.
- Source coverage and review status.
- Rate limits by plan.
- Version history.
- Idempotent artifact generation.
- Signed URLs for private artifacts.

### Entitlement and marginal-cost model

Plan limits must protect actual cost and abuse surfaces without making public research artificially incomplete.

#### Cost-bearing actions

- Private document analysis and model inference.
- OCR, source retrieval, and document parsing.
- Artifact rendering and file storage.
- Alert evaluation and delivery.
- Repository and snapshot storage.
- Authenticated MCP calls.
- Bulk export and institutional API traffic.
- Team activity, audit retention, and support.

#### Low-marginal-cost public actions

- Viewing public records and indexable pages.
- Opening public sources.
- Ordinary public search and filters within abuse protections.
- Viewing public analytics.
- Sharing canonical public links.

#### Entitlement design rules

- Meter expensive actions, persistence, automation, volume, collaboration, and branding control.
- Do not meter access to the factual public record merely to manufacture scarcity.
- Communicate limits before a long operation starts.
- Preserve completed work when a limit is reached.
- Use graceful degradation: branded export, slower queue, fewer watched entities, or no persistence rather than unusable results.
- Track cost per activated user, artifact, private review, alert, MCP workflow, and retained workspace.
- Set final limits only after measuring cost and repeat usage.

### Internationalization and jurisdiction expansion

The schema and URL system must support international growth even if the first refined experience remains US-led.

#### Data requirements

- Original-language title and canonical translated title.
- Original-language source and translated summary status.
- Country-specific court hierarchy and proceeding type.
- Country-specific legal-status and professional-discipline terminology.
- Original amount, currency, conversion rate/date, and normalized reporting amount.
- Local date format while retaining an unambiguous canonical date.
- Jurisdiction-specific citation and docket conventions.
- Translation provenance and reviewer status.

#### Product requirements

- Stable country/jurisdiction URL hierarchy.
- Language alternates only when substantive translated content exists.
- No automatic translation presented as an official quotation.
- Cross-country comparisons disclose doctrinal and procedural differences.
- Financial dashboards separate original currency from normalized analytical views.
- Search resolves native names, translated names, and common aliases to the same entity.

International support is a schema and governance requirement now, not necessarily a full interface deliverable in the first release.

### Build sequence principle

Do not create separate logic for each interface. Build shared record, search, comparison, source, artifact, and repository services; then expose them through web, MCP, and extension adapters.

## 26. Endgame Definition of Done

AI Vortex reaches the intended category position when:

- A legal professional searching a known legal AI incident reliably encounters an AI Vortex page.
- The page answers the immediate question and exposes the underlying evidence.
- The user can compare the record, save it, follow it, or turn it into a review-ready artifact.
- The same object is available through the website, MCP, extension, API, and exports.
- Free users complete meaningful work and are comfortable sharing branded outputs.
- Professional users pay because they value identity, continuity, private workflows, automation, and higher limits.
- Firms pay because the system creates consistency, governance, and an auditable process.
- Judges and researchers can use the product without encountering advocacy disguised as data.
- Vendors and AI skeptics can inspect the same public evidence without the product taking sides.
- Every major claim remains source-linked, versioned, and correctable.
- AI Vortex becomes the place where legal AI risk evidence is found, organized, interpreted, packaged, and redistributed.

That is the endgame: not merely a tracker, plugin, or extension, but the public record and workflow infrastructure for legal AI risk.
